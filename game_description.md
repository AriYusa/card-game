Players manage settlements of characters, developing their skills to overcome challenges. They need to balance personal advancement with collective survival, as the final crisis requires cooperation to prevent everyone from losing.

# Components

Character Cards: Each shows a character with 5 base perks (Combat, Diplomacy, Science, Engineering, Medicine)

Development Cards): Add points to specific perks or grant special abilities

Challenge Cards: Personal objectives requiring specific perk totals

Victory Point Cards: Awarded for completing challenges (can be different depending on challenge), each nominal of 1

Final Crisis Card: The endgame challenge that requires pooled resources (gives no VP)

# Setup
Shuffle all decks separately and place them face-down in the center
Each player draws 3 Character Cards and keeps 2, discarding 1
Each player draws 3 Development Cards

# Round Structure

## Phase 1: Simultanious Draw (done simulationsly, without communicaiton)
Each player draws 1 Challenge Card face-up

## Phase 2: Develop (done simulationsly, without communicaiton)
Draw 1 Character Card OR 3 Development Cards
Play any number of Development Cards on your characters or others' characters.If a character has 5 cards, you can play a 6th, but you must discard one of the existing 5. The discarded card can not be played again or given to other player.

## Phase 3: Challenge check (in turn order, each round the person who strats first changes)
Turn player have right to negotiate help: "I'll boost your Diplomat next round if you help me in my challenge with your Builder” or “i will give you 1 VP if you join me with your character"

Turn player assigness charachters for a challenge (several charachters can be assigned, but the assigned charachers can not be assigned anymore this round, e.g. you can not “provide” a character to other player if it was already assigned, so players should think ahead which they need for themselfs and which can be shared, and how much is the risk)

• Guaranteed Success: If your Character's Perk Total is equal to or greater than the Challenge Requirement, you succeed automatically. No roll needed.

• Risked Success: If your Perk Total is lower than the Requirement, you must roll to bridge the gap.

The Gap (Points Missing),Roll Needed to Succeed (d6),Probability of Success

-2 Points,3 or higher,66%
-3 Points,4 or higher,50%

Debuff: discard Development Cards from each assigned characted (even of other players if they hepled). If character has no develpopmet card, it is Exhausted. Flip the card face-down. It cannot be assigned to challenges for the entire next round.

Helpers share the debuff (discard Development Cards) if you fail, but don't get VP if you succeed (unless negotiated)

Number of rounds = 6 

# Final Crisis

Reveal the Final Crisis Card (requires massive combined perks total 130)

All players have ONE round to simultaniusly:

Negotiate who assigns which characters

Play remaining Development Cards

Pool their characters' perks together

Final case should be that difficult to make it probabelistic (so it is very hard to get a garantied win, but also hard to get garatied lose). Gap and number of rolls can be different from normal round.

## Resolution
Success: Everyone survives! Count individual Victory Points—highest score wins
Failure: Everyone loses the game

# Special Mechanics

## Trading & Deals
Players can trade anything negotiable:
- Development Cards from hand
- Promises of future assistance
- Victory Points (by mutual agreement)
- Strategic character assignments

## Development Card Types
- Permantent Perk Boost (can be plained only during developnmemst phase): a standard Development Card provides an average of +2 points (can be same or different points)

- 1-time Special Ability (can be played any time), exaples:

    - "This character counts for 2 challenges per round"
    - "Reduce another player's character perk by 1" (expect retaliation!)
    - "Can forsee the final challenge"

To determine the exact deck sizes, we need to balance the **Draw Rate** (how many cards players consume) against the **Probability of Variety** (making sure the same cards don't keep appearing).

Assuming a **3-player game** lasting **6 rounds**, here is the mathematical distribution for your components.

---

## 1. Character Deck: 30 Cards

Each base character shoud have strictly 5-6 points in total (randomly, so avg 5.5)
* **Stat Distribution:**
* **15 Specialists:** High base (3) in one perk, 0 or 1 in others. 
* **15 Generalists:** Balanced (1s and 2s) across all perks. 


## 2. Development Deck: 80–90 Cards

This is your most "active" deck because of the **** rule.

* **The Drain:** 3 players drawing 3 cards for even 4 of the 6 rounds = 36 cards. Plus the 9 starting cards = 45 cards.
* **The "Discard" Factor:** Since failures force players to discard these, you need a deep deck so players don't memorize the cards.
* **The "No-Spillover" Mix:**
* **50 Single-Perk (+2):** 10 for each of the 5 perks.
* **30 Dual-Perk (+1/+1):** Essential for the "No-Spillover" rule to help bridge small gaps in two areas.
* **10 One-time Specials:** The "1-time abilities"

## 3. Challenge Deck: 45–50 Cards

Each round, 3 new challenges are drawn. Over 6 rounds, that is 18 cards.

* **Tier Distribution:** * **20 Common** (Tiers 1-2)
* **20 Elite** (Tiers 2-3)
* **20 Heroic** (Tier 4-5)

## 4. Final Crisis Deck: 10 Cards

Each card should have a different "Requirement Profile" (e.g., one is Science-heavy, another is Combat-heavy).
